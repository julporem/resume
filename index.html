Research synthesis · Solo

The four ways AI loses consumer trust

USC · PSYC 550A · Solo project
View presentation

The question

What makes consumers trust AI in the marketplace, and where exactly does that trust fail?

What I did

Synthesized the consumer-AI research on anthropomorphism, affect, and trust into a working model of trust failure.

What I found

Four failure modes: feeling surveilled, being misclassified, fear of replacement, and emotionally tone-deaf responses. The last one does the most damage.

So what: warmth is AI's weakest dimension and its biggest driver of attachment. That gap became my treatise.
Qualitative · Solo

AI at work: everyone uses it, nobody has rules for it

USC · PSYC 509 · Solo project
View report

The question

How are employees across industries actually using AI on the job?

What I did

4 in-depth interviews (60 to 90 minutes each) and 5 hours of ethnographic observation across 3 work settings, with a structured observation checklist.

What I found

AI is already built into daily routines. Concerns about privacy and accuracy sit right alongside daily use. Not one participant had a written AI policy at work.

So what: the barrier isn't the technology, it's the lack of shared norms. Four recommendations, starting with written AI policies by context.
Quantitative · Team of 2

Ketel One is talking to the wrong drinker

USC · Segmentation review · I led the project and designed the deck
View deck

The question

What actually drives vodka choice, and where should a heritage brand position itself?

What I did

Principal components analysis on 47 benefit statements (N=801) to find 8 choice factors, then cluster analysis to find 7 segments.

What I found

The heritage messaging misses what the best segment values: taste. Personal Taste Seekers order premium 84% of the time, and they already prefer Ketel One at more than double the market rate.

So what: reposition around private, personal taste. Creative direction: "Good taste doesn't need an audience."
UX research · Team of 3

YouTube Shorts: why people use it, and where it trips them up

USC · Two linked studies · I analyzed the data and wrote the findings
Foundational study Usability study

The question

Shorts gets over a billion views a day but barely converts viewers to long-form. Why do people pick it, and can they get back to what they liked?

What we did

In-depth interviews with Shorts users and non-users, then a task-based usability test on saving and revisiting Shorts.

What I found

Platform choice follows existing habits. And the bottleneck is saving, not finding: Save is buried in a menu, and several participants didn't know it existed.

So what: put Save on the main Shorts sidebar and keep saved Shorts separate from long-form content.
